Vented front brakes for the Touring, Alpina style (part 1)

A used vented-brake package rebuilt on the kitchen table: ti struts, E21 323i hubs with oversize studs, nickel-plated 2002 Turbo calipers, Neue Klasse shields cut to scoop air like Alpina did, and NOS BALO discs.

The idea

The 02 came with solid front discs; only the 2002 Turbo had vented ones. A common way to get vented brakes on a ti/tii-type front end is to use E21 323i hubs and discs with 2002 Turbo (or E12) four-piston calipers on ti/tii struts. In September 2026 I bought a used package with most of what that needs, and in October I rebuilt it so it can go straight onto the Touring.

What came in the box

The package, bought in September 2026: two ti/tii struts, two E21 big-bearing hubs already drilled for the 02's four-stud pattern and fitted with 2002 rear-hub knurled studs, two used vented discs, two four-piston calipers with the right spacing, and a pair of handmade aluminium shields from the previous owner.

Struts

The struts had been powder-coated years ago by the previous owner and were still in good shape, so they only needed a very thorough clean. I measured the distance between the caliper mounting points to confirm that they really are ti struts: ti/tii/Turbo struts have the wider 88.9 mm caliper mounts, the "small" 02s 76.2 mm.

Hubs: new studs

The hubs had radial play at the studs: the knurled studs no longer sat tight in their holes. I cleaned the hubs ultrasonically and derusted them completely, then pressed in oversize knurled studs, Febi 33499 (M12×1.5, grade 10.9), which grip properly in the worn holes. No press needed: I pulled each stud in with a conical wheel nut. First with the conical side against the hub, so the stud centres itself in the hole, then turned round to the flat side with a couple of greased washers underneath. After that: 1K etch primer and 1K black.

Checking run-out

With the hubs on the struts I checked the run-out of the hub face with a dial indicator: about 0.05 mm on one hub and around 0.04 mm on the other.

Calipers: new for the car, old ones restored for sale

At Veterama in October I found a restored, nickel-plated pair of ATE 2002 Turbo calipers in excellent condition. These go on the car.

The old calipers from the package were stripped completely, derusted in citric acid in the ultrasonic cleaner, and wire-brushed. The pistons cleaned up well. They still need paint, and then they will be sold, together with the previous owner's aluminium shields.

Shields: Neue Klasse, Alpina style

Alpina fitted Neue Klasse disc shields on the 02 because their shape scoops air onto the disc. I bought a new pair (BMW 34 11 2 760 114) and cut them with a Dremel so they fit behind the 02 strut. The previous owner's handmade aluminium shields were stripped and painted black; they will go with the old calipers.

Discs: NOS BALO

The used discs from the package were checked, but for the car I found a pair of new-old-stock BALO discs for the E21 323i.

Where it stands

By 22 October both corners were mocked up: strut, hub, Turbo caliper and shield. The project is not finished yet. Still to do:

- New FAG wheel bearings in the hubs (bought from Autodoc in 2025). - Fit the zinc-plated brake-hose brackets, the yellow-zinc hose clips and the M12 wave washers (Kapteijn). - Make new CuNiFe brake lines. - Pads: Textar (already on the shelf), with a new Bosch pad fitting kit. - Fit everything to the Touring.

The ti struts currently on the car, with calipers restored in September 2025, then stay on the shelf as a plug-in alternative. Part two of this story follows when the brakes are on the car.

Twin Webers in two weeks, just in time for Voula

An untouched pair of Weber 40 DCOE 76/77 from my father's warehouse, rebuilt and fitted under an Alpina air box in two weeks, finished the day before the Voula hillclimb.

The deadline

The Voula hillclimb was on 25 April 2026, and I wanted the Touring there on twin side-draught Webers instead of the original single Solex carb of a 2002 2.0. That left about two weeks, and it turned out to be a hell of a job. I did it together with my father, in his yard in Athens.

The carbs

The carbs are an original, untouched pair of Weber 40 DCOE 76/77 that turned up in my father's warehouse. They cost nothing, but they needed a full rebuild before they could go anywhere near the engine.

Rebuilding them

I stripped both carbs completely and cleaned every part thoroughly. Every jet, emulsion tube and small part went into its own cup so nothing got mixed up between the two carbs. They went back together with new gasket sets. The spindle bearings were in excellent condition, so I left them in.

The jetting is the factory setting for the 40 DCOE 76/77 (from the Weber application table for the Alfa Romeo 2000, a 1962 cc four-cylinder like the M10): choke 32, auxiliary venturi 4.5, main jet 135, air corrector 210, emulsion tube F34, idle jet 55 F17, starter 85 F9, pump jet 35, needle valve 150.

Manifolds, gaskets and coolant

The intake manifolds were cleaned and polished; no port matching was needed. Between head and manifold went new paper gaskets, and between manifold and carb new phenolic spacers with two paper gaskets each (Walloth & Nesch). All washers and nuts are new. The manifold-to-carb nuts are 13 mm instead of the original 12 mm, because we could not find genuine 12 mm ones from BMW. That makes tightening the carbs onto the manifolds a bit trickier.

For the coolant I used a cleaned original 2002ti water divider. The metal bypass tube that a twin-carb engine needs came from an E30 318i M10 with L-Jetronic, with its one small outlet plugged. This way the heater hose on the firewall stays exactly as it was, so going back to a single carb would be easy.

Linkage and throttle

The original 2002ti throttle linkage was in excellent condition and went straight back on. The bridge-type bracket that bolts onto the manifold studs was raised with two thick washers, so the linkage clears the chokes. The DCOE-specific lever arms I did not have; I bought them separately from Athanasakos.

The firewall bracket that supports the linkage got a new bushing, softened in warm water to fit, which took the noise away. A throttle return spring runs from the left carb lever to the stud on the side of the manifold, as on an original 2002ti.

The rod from the pedal box to the carb linkage had to be straightened. The ti has its own firewall bracket, which pushes the rod to the side, and a bent rod then hits the gearbox and clutch. The ti originally has a straight rod, but we could not find one. The clamps at both ends of the rod are new.

The Alpina air box

The air filter housing came with the original Alpina A1 Solex DDH set I bought; we restored it in Delft in February, and it now carries Alpina lettering from Walloth & Nesch. The same set also gave the polished intake funnels and a second linkage. They are needed because the air filter tubes are inclined, and they make the connection between carbs and box. The box is held on one of the strut tower studs with a bracket that is also original to the A1 setup.

Into the car

On 20 April the old induction came off, the ports were covered, and the manifolds and carbs went on. The next days were for the linkage, fuel lines and the air box.

Finished, one day early

By 24 April the Alpina box sat on its bracket and the engine bay looked as if it had always been that way. The next morning we drove to Voula.

Later that summer: the right distributor

In the summer I fitted a good original Bosch 0 231 151 008 (JFU 4), the centrifugal-only distributor of the 2002 ti and tii. It was my father's old stock, kept aside for this car. I set it up following the factory manual and Wolfgang's guidance on the 02-club forum:

- At idle the timing is at TDC. - 25° BTDC at 2,200 rpm, with the vacuum hose disconnected. (For the 0 231 151 008, -009, -003, 0 231 188 002, -001 and 0 231 129 037; only the 0 231 129 026 reaches 25° already at 1,400 rpm.) On the pulley that is about 25 mm from the TDC mark. - Full advance of 30–34° BTDC at 3,500 rpm, about 32 mm from TDC on the pulley.

The settings for a standard ti apply to engines up to a 304° camshaft.

How it runs

In general it behaves well and gives a good baseline. There is some spitting back through the carbs at low load around 2,200 rpm, so the first thing to try is a slightly richer idle jet: from 55 F17 to 60 F17.

Where it started

The wheel came to me in August 2025: a three-spoke Ferrero with polished slotted spokes and a wooden rim that had seen decades of sun and hands. The spokes still carry the engraved Ferrero signature, but the wood was grey, dry and cracked along the joints.

Taking it apart

In January the hub and horn contact came off. In February the rim itself came apart: underneath the wood there is a thin metal hoop riveted to the spokes, and the wood is two grooved rings glued around it from either side. The old glue had let go in several places, which is where the cracks came from.

Gluing it back together

The kitchen table became the workshop. The half-rims went back onto the hoop with fresh glue and were clamped the old-fashioned way: dozens of turns of twine, pulled tight all around the rim, left overnight to cure.

Back to bare wood

Next the rim was sanded back to bare, light wood — taking the old finish and the grey layer off completely. That also showed every crack and open grain, which got filled where the rim meets the spokes.

Colour and finish

With the wood clean, the colour went back on in thin layers, building from a light tone to a deep walnut. Between coats the wheel hung from the ceiling to dry. The final touch was a German furniture wax, rubbed in by hand.

The result

The whole job ran from early February to mid-March 2026, mostly in the evenings. I documented it in more detail in the bmw2002faq steering wheel thread (link below).

Why this part

For the twin-carb setup on the Touring I want the original 2002ti air filter housing: the long black drum with four rubber boots to the carbs and a round filter at each end. This one arrived in January 2026 — complete, but with chipped paint and surface rust.

This housing came as part of the original Alpina A1 Solex DDH set I bought, together with the intake funnels, a mounting bracket and a second linkage. A few months later I bought a second 2002ti housing in good condition.

Cleaning and paint

Everything came apart. The drum got a thorough wash (yes, it went through the dishwasher), the inside was painted first, then the outside in satin black. The small hardware was cleaned separately.

Reassembly

Back together with the rubber boots, clamps, carb adapters, the mounting bracket and the long through-rod that holds the end caps.

In April it went onto the Touring, with Alpina lettering, on top of a pair of Weber 40 DCOE 76/77 (see the twin-Weber story).

The starting point

In January 2026 I had a 2002 ti/tii 220 km/h VDO cluster on the bench: three round instruments in a black housing, original black faces, and a housing whose textured paint had worn through on the edges.

Housing: masking and airbrushing

The instruments came out and the housing was masked inside and out. The paint went on with an airbrush in thin layers to keep the original texture visible.

Bezels and rings

The inner bezels and retaining rings were cleaned and repainted gloss black, the chrome trim rings polished.

The silver-dollar faces

The key step: masking the black faces so that only the centre discs are exposed, then spraying them silver — the look of the early 2002 ti "silver dollar" clusters.

Back together

Reassembled the same night. The next day it got its first photos in daylight.

A second cluster, and market prices

A week later a second cluster arrived.

Lights on

In March both clusters were tested with the illumination on, and the housing of the second one was stripped for the same treatment.
