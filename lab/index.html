Theoretical limit for the selected setup: full throttle, ideal gear changes, the cornering limit from the model in every bend and the braking limit before it; dry road, no driver margin. Roads come from OpenStreetMap (routing by OSRM), heights from Open-Meteo (Copernicus DEM, 90 m); the elevation is smoothed. Lines: the own-lane and racing lines are minimum-curvature lines within the lane or the whole road (closed road); the road width is not in the map data, so set it. Hover over the route to see the lines up close. Shift points follow practical rules (change down under braking at ≤ 5500 rpm, no gear held under 1.5 s; both are sliders in the Powertrain tab); the lap time assumes ideal shifting. Braking is limited by tyre grip and the brake balance; brake fade is not modelled. Circuits (a route that finishes where it starts) run as a flying lap: the car crosses the line at the speed it finishes the lap with, and the lines wrap round the start/finish. Untick “flying lap” for a standing start. Each road file can carry its own width (picked up when you choose it).

Lap by lap

The brake session uses the road, line, width and start of the Road tab. Each lap is driven at the limit with ideal braking, the disc and drum temperatures carried over from the lap before; open roads run once. Pad and lining friction fall with temperature (curves in baseline.yaml), hot brakes give less torque and shift the front/rear split; when the torque runs short the lap slows. Most brake inputs are estimates (pedal ratio, booster, pad curve, cooling): read the trends and the comparison between setups (solid vs vented discs, road vs race pads), not exact degrees. A real driver brakes earlier than the model, so the time lost to fade here is a minimum.

Damper curves are placeholders (confidence D) until the real Koni data are in; the tyre is a generic 185/70 R13 road tyre. Handling diagram: steering beyond the Ackermann angle against lateral acceleration; a rising line means understeer.
